  - How can resonant, all-dielectric metasurfaces enhance weak optical effects such as magneto-optical Faraday rotation?
  - How can metasurface lattices be designed to host guided and topological modes with controllable spin and valley properties?
  - How can metasurfaces be monolithically combined with semiconductor devices such as photodetectors?

Metasurfaces are planar arrays of subwavelength scatterers whose collective response can be engineered far beyond what a bulk material offers. My work uses them in two ways: as **resonant enhancers** of weak optical effects (for example, electromagnetically induced transparency boosting Faraday rotation in all-dielectric magneto-optical metasurfaces), and as **lattices** whose band structure is designed to support topological and spin-dependent guided modes.

A recurring theme is the link between design and fabrication: structures are modeled with full-wave simulation (FDTD, RCWA, FEM) before being realized in silicon and group-IV material platforms, including Si/Ge heterostructure metasurfaces for wavelength-selective photodetection.
