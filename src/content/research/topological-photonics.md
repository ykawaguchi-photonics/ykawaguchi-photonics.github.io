  - Can synthetic gauge fields and geometric phases be observed and exploited on a silicon chip?
  - How can topological principles yield resonators whose spectrum is stable regardless of cavity shape?

Topological photonics borrows ideas from condensed-matter physics to build optical structures whose key properties are protected by global, topological features of the band structure. This makes it possible to guide light along boundaries in ways that are robust to certain defects, and to give light additional synthetic degrees of freedom — pseudo-spin and valley.

My research includes heterogeneous topological boundary modes that convert between pseudo-spin and valley polarization (with an Aharonov–Bohm-type demonstration on a silicon chip), spin-dependent guided modes in photonic Dirac metasurfaces, topological polaritonic structures in transition-metal-dichalcogenide crystals, and freeform topological vortex resonators with spectrally stable modes.
